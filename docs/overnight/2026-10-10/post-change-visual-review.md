## Gugeun and Jinhyeon: side-glass correction accepted within its scope

Sources: [Gugeun detailed original](../../../references/research-20261010/foliage-source/facility-review/gugeun-detail.jpg) and [Jinhyeon original](../../../references/research-20261010/foliage-source/facility-review/jinhyeon.jpg). The supplementary oblique renders show thin dark returns beside the projecting front panes, rather than only a broad white side closure. This is the appropriate correction to the source discrepancy. Projection depth was already present before this change: do not describe it as newly added or formerly absent.

Final map03/map06 remain high and almost frontal. Their windows still read largely as flat dark rectangles at this screen scale. That is insufficient evidence to call the projection absent, and does not justify increasing its depth. The 0.55m projection and 0.40m side pane remain estimates. Final facade proportions, window divisions, canopy/stair details and roof profiles are still schematic compared with the photographs; plain wall/glass rendering and lack of source vegetation reinforce that simplification.

Acceptance here is limited to exposing the source-supported side-glass region. A matched, close three-quarter final capture is still needed before claiming the window assemblies reproduce the original proportions.

## Graduate School: open topology improved, entrance not complete

Sources: [official front](../../../references/graduate-school.jpg) and [4032px 2024 original](../../../references/research-20261010/high-resolution-search/target-223408506698-19.jpg). They support an open dark entrance under a tiled canopy. The front original shows two conspicuous round canopy supports; the larger 2024 photograph confirms rounded main colonnade columns, though vegetation obscures much of the entrance.

The open-canopy path removes the enclosed porch body and inappropriate generic decoration above the canopy. In the phase33 close/oblique captures, a narrow view through the canopy to the main facade is now visible. The former pale slab above the small roof is absent in final map13. These are real improvements.

The still-pale rectangle beneath the roof in final map13 is not enough to conclude that the enclosed body remains: from that high viewpoint, the retained floor's top and rear facade overlap the opening. The low captures distinguish the open space from this camera occlusion.

Concrete remaining defects are more visible once the enclosure is removed:

- Four narrow square front supports remain, contrasting with the two substantial round front supports visible in the source. Main facade columns are also square in the model despite their clearly round source profiles.
- The canopy is offset from its own intended entry sequence. Data center the canopy at local E=7.5 while the main doorway/stairs are centered at E=12.15: a 4.65m model offset. The oblique capture shows the broad doorway and stairs displaced to the right of the canopy. The source supports a coherent canopy/door/step relationship, but these numbers are model coordinates, not surveyed source dimensions.
- The retained floor is an elevated shallow slab; the posts continue beneath it. In the close capture, left support ends visibly terminate above the terrain, while right supports overlap the stairs. Their ground support cannot be accepted from this image. The open-canopy generator preserves post base 0 while the slab top is approximately 0.805 in part-local height; it does not establish a continuous threshold-to-landing route. The actual terrain gap has not been measured by this reviewer.
- Roof, landing and stairs do not form a convincing single entrance assembly. Generic upper balcony/glazing and source-specific round columns remain outside this correction.

Do not describe Graduate School as a completed entrance reconstruction. Next work should use the front original and an unobstructed side/entrance photograph to define one aligned canopy, threshold, landing, steps and supports contract. Resolve slab and terrain contact together, then verify low front/side views. Do not infer an additional basement, relocate the whole building, or invent a cardinal assignment to compensate.

## Haku: plaque accepted; short stairs, shore and timber details remain

Sources: original [hak02](../../../references/research-20261010/panorama/photo/hak/hak02.jpg) and [hak03](../../../references/research-20261010/panorama/photo/hak/hak03.jpg), both directly re-inspected. Their access side has a dark Hanja plaque with a muted gold frame, short pale stone steps, substantial irregular timber details and a shore immediately around the access area.

The phase35 plaque-front and plaque-corner captures show the complete photographed frame and three light characters. The modeled rafter ends no longer cross the lettering. The revised crop and lower receiver are accepted as a small visual correction. The original 853×480 image supplies only roughly 58×43 plaque pixels, so the enlarged board is visibly soft. No sharp-lettering or exact placement claim is justified. Its approximately 1.20×0.55m receiver remains an estimate.

The current plaque hangs somewhat below the simplified beam, whereas the source board sits against a deeper, more articulated timber beam/lintel assembly. Lowering it successfully cleared the current rafters; it did not reconstruct that supporting timber. The phase36 map10 view faces another side and does not show the access-face plaque. That is a view-selection limitation, not evidence that the plaque disappeared.

All six phase35 captures retain the square pavilion, open rail system, white piers and curved tiled roof. The compact rafters have no obvious through-roof protrusion in these views, consistent with the separately reported clearance check. Roof fidelity, exact rafter counts and joinery are not established by that engineering check. In particular, the nominal underside capture is still too distant to audit hidden joinery.

Major remaining differences:

- The current approximately 5.885m level approach slab is much longer than the short stone-step approach visible in hak02/03. The polygonal pond/shore relationship also differs visibly. This is a real source mismatch.
- **Do not call the current endpoint detached from the terrain.** It has an explicit photo-estimated grading patch in `src/data/site-grading.ts`, outline `[[6.8,-1.2],[11,-.5],[11,3.5],[6.8,3]]`, height 1.49. The coordinator's public `globe.getHeight` samples at endpoint center/edges were 1.4880–1.4885 versus slab bottom 1.49: a roughly 1.5–2mm residual within the estimated model grid, not a physical survey. The dark area below the span does not invalidate that endpoint contact.
- Shortening the slab would move its endpoint outside the current supported patch and alter its relation to the pond outline. A new landing cannot be declared grounded by reusing the existing endpoint result. Follow the bounded [approach contract](haku-approach-source-contract.md); obtain a supported shoreline/landing relation before changing the patch.
- Source beams have shallow carved/scalloped lintels, shaped corner brackets, conspicuous natural grain, joints and irregularities. The current smooth beams, small geometric brackets, uniform ochre surfaces and repeated rail motif are schematic. The photo plaque does not fix those differences. The unaccepted dark stock-wood trial is not evidence of improved fidelity.

Priority is the access/shore relationship, followed by source-supported timber silhouette details. Use additional close source views or measurements for steps/landing and beam profiles. Do not fabricate floral/animal carvings, guess submerged banks, or change the verified anchor/water datum just to improve one screenshot.

## Boin: camera framing corrected; blank faces remain unverified

Sources: [official facility image](../../../references/boinjae.jpg), [2024 parking-side photo](../../../references/research-20261010/astra/blog-20240711_132106.jpg), and [2024 lawn-side photo](../../../references/research-20261010/astra/blog-20240731_153340.jpg), all directly re-inspected.

Final map14 now contains both principal blocks, all their roof edges, the linking area and useful ground context. The selected building no longer clips against the foreground as it did in phase27. The visible glazing corner and windowed facade make the building more identifiable. This is an accepted desktop selected-view usability correction. The source camera's exact geographical azimuth remains unknown; the new view is a useful composition, not a verified photographic heading.

The low connector is only partly readable between the blocks. The nearer block has an extensive blank side, and both visible faces of the farther block appear almost entirely blank. The available 2024 photos show broad brick areas but also numerous windows, vertical glazing strips and the low glazed connection. The correspondence between every model face and every photographed face is not verified. Therefore the blank model faces are **unverified placeholders**, not proven blank source walls, but they must not be filled with invented repeated windows either.

Next work requires a face-by-face source map tied to the contemporary footprint, lawn/parking levels and confirmed orientation. Obtain current oblique/aerial evidence or a plan before changing openings on unseen faces. Verify a complementary entry/connector view after mapping. No new mobile capture was reviewed here, so desktop framing acceptance does not certify 375px behavior.
